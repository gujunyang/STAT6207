History of Machine Learning (1950s-1960s)
• The Dartmouth Workshop (1956): brought together researchers who aimed 
to simulate human intelligence using computers. 
• Early Neural Networks: developed the concept of perceptrons, which were 
basic artificial neurons capable of learning. However, perceptrons had 
limitations, and interest in neural networks declined due to their inability to 
solve complex problems.
33
[IMAGES] 4

=====PAGE 33=====
History of Machine Learning (1960s-1980s)
• Symbolic AI and Expert Systems: using explicit rules and logic to solve 
problems with knowledge bases and reasoning engines
• The Backpropagation Algorithm (1986): The backpropagation algorithm, 
developed by Geoffrey Hinton, David Rumelhart, and Ronald Williams, 
allowed for efficient training of multi-layered neural networks
34
[IMAGES] 3

=====PAGE 34=====
History of Machine Learning (1990s- 2010s)
• Support Vector Machines (SVMs): popular for classify data by finding optimal 
decision boundaries. 
• Rise of Big Data and Data-Driven Approaches: led to increased interest in 
data-driven approaches, such as decision trees, random forests, and 
ensemble methods.
• Deep Learning and Neural Networks: multiple layers, achieved remarkable 
success in various domains, including computer vision, natural language 
processing, and speech recognition.
35
[IMAGES] 2

=====PAGE 35=====
History of Machine Learning (2010 – 2020)
• ImageNet Breakthrough (AlexNet): won the ImageNet competition by a wide 
margin using a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) trained on GPUs. This 
event catalyzed the modern deep learning boom and shifted computer 
vision away from hand-engineered features.
• Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs):  a generator network against a 
discriminator network in a zero-sum game framework, enabling realistic 
image synthesis and data augmentation.
• The Transformer Architecture: by replacing recurrent structures with multi-
head self-attention mechanisms, Transformers enabled massive 
parallelization during training and became the foundational architecture for 
modern NLP and vision.
36
[IMAGES] 2

=====PAGE 36=====
History of Machine Learning (2020+)
• OpenAI introduced GPT-3 (175 billion parameters), demonstrating that 
scaling autoregressive Transformer models enables strong few-shot and 
zero-shot learning capabilities across diverse language tasks without task-
specific fine-tuning.
• Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs) surpassed GANs in visual 
generation quality. Systems like Latent Diffusion (Stable Diffusion) made 
high-fidelity text-to-image synthesis efficient and accessible.
• OpenAI released ChatGPT, pairing Large Language Models with 
Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF). This unlocked highly 
coherent instruction-following and conversational interactions, triggering 
widespread enterprise and consumer adoption.
37
[IMAGES] 2

=====PAGE 37=====
History of Machine Learning (2020+)
• Machine learning shifted toward natively multimodal architectures capable 
of processing text, vision, audio, and code simultaneously. Advances in 
reasoning-focused architectures, Mixture of Experts (MoE), parameter-
efficient fine-tuning (PEFT)
• Agentic Workflow: Decomposes complex tasks via chain-of-thought, tree-of-
thought, or self-reflection loops to correct errors dynamically. Implemented 
the capacity to execute external tools including code interpreters, web 
search, API endpoints, databases, and filesystem operations.

Noise and Model Complexity 
• Sources of noise:
• Measurement (input)
• Labelling (output)
• Hidden attributes
• Simple rectangle (linear) model is preferred
• Simpler to use with lower computational complexity
• Easier to train with lower space complexity
• Easier to explain with higher interpretability
• Better generalization with lower variance (Occam’s razor)

=====PAGE 52=====
Model selection
• Learning is an ill-posed problem
• Data alone is not sufficient to find a unique solution
• Inductive bias (i.e., assumptions about H) is needed. 
• Generalization: how well a learned model performs on new data. 
• Overfitting: H is more complex than C 
• Underfitting: H is less complex than C 
• Model selection: choosing the right inductive bias.
